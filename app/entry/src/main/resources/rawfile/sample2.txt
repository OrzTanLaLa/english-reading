Coffee was once a mystery. Nobody knew why it kept people awake — or why it tasted so bitter. In 1820, a French chemist named Pierre-Jean Robiquet isolated caffeine, the compound responsible for both effects. It is a small molecule, and it works by blocking adenosine receptors in the brain. The result is not extra energy; it is the temporary absence of the signal that says "you are tired." That distinction matters, because a cup of coffee cannot repay a debt of sleep — it can only hide the bill for a while.
